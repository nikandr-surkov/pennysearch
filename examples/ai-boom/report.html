Is the AI boom making money yet?

How does AI spending compare with AI revenue in 2026, and is anyone profitable?

The short answer

Not yet overall, but the revenue is arriving faster than ever. The biggest US tech firms plan to spend up to $725 billion on AI data centers in 2026 1. Anthropic's revenue run rate is approaching $110 billion 19 and OpenAI's was about $50 billion in September 23. Anthropic now reports an adjusted profit 35, yet Gartner expects at most $3.6 trillion of AI revenue through 2030 against about $7 trillion of spending 47.

What you need to know

  • Spending: up to $725 billion of capex planned for 2026 by the biggest US tech firms 12, and analysts expect more than $1 trillion in 2027 12.
  • The fastest revenue ramp yet: Anthropic went from a $9 billion run rate at the end of 2025 to $65 billion in July 17, and to almost $110 billion by October 19.
  • OpenAI was at about $50 billion a year at the end of September 23 and aims for $70 billion or more by year-end 24.
  • Cloud is where AI pays first: Google Cloud grew 82% to $24.8 billion in the second quarter 26 and AWS 37% to $42.2 billion 25.
  • Profit comes with an asterisk: Anthropic reports two adjusted-profit quarters 35, but on standard accounting it lost $42 billion in 2025 40.
  • The bear case: Gartner puts more than $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure at risk of write-downs by 2030 48, and about a third of AI capex now comes from debt 51.
  • For the companies buying AI, half now get value from it (BCG) 57, but they expect 28 months on average to see it (Gallagher) 58.

Where the money goes

Spending plans kept rising all year. In April the biggest US tech firms planned up to $725 billion of 2026 capex 1. After the July calls, the four largest cloud providers guided $720-745 billion combined 2.

  • Microsoft gave a first 2026 estimate of $190 billion 3. Its lower-looking July figure is a lease-accounting change, not less building 4.
  • Amazon raised its 2026 cash capex to about $220 billion, mostly because of memory prices 8.
  • Alphabet raised to $180-190 billion in April 5 and again in July 6.
  • Meta guides $130-145 billion 10, about double the $72 billion it spent in 2025 9.

The first quarter alone set a record of $130 billion 11, and analysts expect more than $1 trillion in 2027 12.

Where the money comes from

The labs' revenue is growing faster than any software business before them, but most numbers are self-reported or come from people familiar with them.

  • Anthropic: $4.7 billion in the first quarter, $11.6 billion in the second and $16.8 billion in the third 151413. The run rate went from $9 billion to $65 billion between December and July 17, with more than $100 billion expected this year 18.
  • OpenAI: more than $20 billion a year at the end of 2025 22, over $40 billion in August 21 and about $50 billion in September 23.

The two labs may not measure run rate the same way 16. One reported OpenAI figure near $70 billion used Anthropic's method for sales through cloud providers 24, so the numbers are not directly comparable.

Cloud: the first place AI pays

The clearest profits so far are at the cloud providers that rent out the chips. Google Cloud's operating income more than tripled to $8.8 billion in the second quarter 27, and the unit is now about 21% of Alphabet's revenue 30. AWS had its fastest growth since 2021 25. Order books point the same way: Google's backlog reached $462 billion 32, and Jefferies puts the cloud providers' combined backlog near $2 trillion 33.

Profit, with an asterisk

Anthropic says it was profitable on an adjusted basis for a second straight quarter 35, with an 11% adjusted operating margin and positive free cash flow in the third quarter 37. Each dollar it spends on compute brings in $1.70 of revenue 38.

The adjustments matter. They strip out stock-based compensation 35. The reported gross margins come before revenue shared with partners such as Amazon and before training costs 39. On standard accounting the company lost $42 billion in 2025 40, and it has committed to at least $518 billion of compute spending over the next decade 42. Across the industry, AI-native companies run gross margins of 50-65%, against 75-85% for classic software 46.

The bear case

Gartner's math is the starkest: about $7 trillion of AI infrastructure spending through 2030 against at most $3.6 trillion of AI revenue 47, with more than $3.5 trillion at risk of write-downs 48. Bain estimates the industry needs $6 trillion a year in revenue by 2031 50.

The financing is getting tighter too. About a third of AI capex now comes from debt 51. AI-related debt issuance fell from $113 billion in June to $23 billion in September 52, and CoreWeave's bond yields rose above 15% 53. The critic Ed Zitron points to Microsoft: $34.33 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2026, $24.1 billion of it from OpenAI, against $261 billion of capex since 2022 54.

Inside the companies that buy AI

For the companies using it, the picture is positive but slow. Half now generate value from AI 57, and 82% report positive impacts, but they expect an average of 28 months to realise the value 58. Strongly positive returns are concentrated among leaders who measure growth, not only efficiency 56.

Where sources disagree

How much did Anthropic earn in 2025?

The draft IPO prospectus shows $4.6 billion of revenue on standard accounting 40. CNBC reported roughly $10 billion for the year 59. Neither source explains the gap; it is likely a different way of counting revenue.

Keep in mind

  • Most of the labs' revenue and profit figures are self-reported, unaudited, or come from people familiar with the matter.
  • A run rate is the latest month or quarter times 12. It is not the year's revenue.
  • "Adjusted" profit leaves out costs such as stock-based compensation.
  • Two Bloomberg pages were paywalled, so only their teasers were read (marked short page).
